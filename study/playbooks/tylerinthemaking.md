# What @tylerinthemaking does, and how Align does it

Studied from all 42 of his reels (Feb 18 – Oct 22, 2026), with transcripts,
on-screen text, stills, likes and comments. The pack is in the private repo
`hakeemawbrey/align-study`. Instagram gave no view counts, so "× typical" here
means likes against his median reel (16K likes, 138 comments).

## The page in one paragraph

Tyler (204K followers, SF, "becoming a modern-day gentleman, husband-to-be")
posts one thing: a 45-second talking head about how a man should show up for
the woman he's with. He sits at the same desk in front of the same painting
every time. A big headline sits at the top for the whole video, with one-line
captions low on screen. Every video rests on his own seven-year relationship
with his fiancée, and most open with a study ("So I recently read that…").
He posts about 1.3 times a week, mornings Central. He is a man talking to men,
but the numbers say women are the ones liking and sending it: hooks aimed at
women do ×1.1, hooks aimed at men ×0.6.

## His three formats that work

**1. "The Gentleman's Corner": an advice question (6 posts, median ×8.3; 4 of his top 8)**

His current breakout (Aug–Oct). It runs about 40 seconds:

1. The question is on screen in a dark message box, written the way people type: "my gf ___ but I ___. what did I do wrong?" He reads it out.
2. Then the title: "Welcome back to the Gentleman's Corner. Today, let's talk about ___."
3. He's kind to the asker first ("You had good intentions…"), then reframes: *she wasn't doing X, she was doing Y.*
4. He gives one concrete fix, often a script to say out loud ("ask her if we're venting or fixing").
5. A one-line rule to finish.

The asker is always the man in the wrong but meaning well. The comments fill up because everyone has an opinion on the asker (2.2K on his best one).

**2. "You're not husband material yet": a stat with a twist (7 posts with this hook, ×19 at best, ×0.2 at worst; the same stat-and-twist shape runs through about half his page)**

Hook: "if she ___, you're not husband material yet". Then:
- A study: "So I recently read that if a woman marries a man, …"
- The twist: "But if you're a man trying to change that, the last thing you want to do is *help her out*." He sets up the obvious answer, then rules it out.
- "Welcome to episode N of Becoming a Modern-Day Gentleman."
- Proof from his own home ("I own those chores from start to finish"), then a rule.

The format is the same every time; **the topic decides the result.** Housework (×19), her stress (×6.4) and kids (×3.1) won. "Protecting her health" (×0.2) and "solving her feelings" (×0.3) didn't.

**3. "2 things I didn't realize were misogynistic (as a man)": a confession list (4 posts, median ×4.0)**

"I used to think ___ was easy, but these are two things I subconsciously did…". Two numbered examples, each "I used to ___. I realized that's a problem because ___. Now I ___." Owning his own mistakes makes it safe to share.

## What doesn't work for him

- **Reacting to or stitching other creators:** ×0.2–×0.5 (the 50/50 rebuttal, "marriage ruins your life", the success-ego one).
- **Classic dating advice for men:** approaching women ×0.1, dating apps ×0.1, "nice guys" ×0.2.
- **Manifestos about himself:** "I'm going to replace the Manosphere", ×0.2.
- **Hooks aimed at men** ("fellas", "as a guy") do about half as well as hooks aimed at women.
- **Calls to action barely matter here.** He rarely asks for anything; the content gets shared on its own.

## The craft, to copy exactly

- **30–60 seconds.** 90% of his reels; his two at 60–90 seconds did ×3.3.
- **The headline is the hook, and it stays up the whole video.** Large serif type, top third, two lines.
- **Spoken captions:** a few words at a time, low on screen.
- **A series title card about 5 seconds in** ("Becoming a Modern Day Gentleman · Episode 008"). Every video says it's part of something.
- **One set, one take, eye level, soft light.** No b-roll except an occasional GIF.
- **A number in the first 5 seconds** ("7 hours", "3 times more likely", "70%").
- **His own relationship is the proof every time** ("my fiancée and I, seven years").
- **End on a rule you could screenshot:** "If money covers the roof, commitment builds the home."

## How Align does it

Same structure, Align's subject: **what to do before and during dating so it goes right**, read through your chart. Host: Hakeem on camera (Founder Studio scripts and teleprompter in `founder/`). Voice as in `founder/data/align-brief.json`: calm, honest, no hype, astrology *reads*, never decides, Align mentioned at most twice and never sold. Two things Tyler does that Align must not:

- **No percent-match framing.** His "fair match calculator" is a percent; Align has no compatibility score by design.
- **Don't invent questions or studies.** Use real questions from DMs, comments or the app; until there are enough, say "a question I get a lot" rather than presenting one as a follower's. Every stat needs its source, and anything not checked yet is left in [brackets].

### Format A: "Say the honest thing" (Align's Gentleman's Corner)

The question on screen in a message-style box (Align colours, not black). The answer: be kind first, reframe, read the sign in one true line, give the honest sentence to say, and end on a rule.

> **On screen:** "she's a scorpio and went quiet for two days after I joked about her ex. I said sorry. why is she still cold?"
>
> **Said:** [Read the question.] This is Say the Honest Thing. Today: when sorry isn't the end of it.
> You meant it as a joke, and you did apologise. But she isn't punishing you. Scorpio reads tone before words, and what she heard was that her past is something you'd laugh about. The silence is her deciding whether that's safe to show you.
> Don't send another sorry. Send the honest thing: "I made light of something that matters to you. I want to know about it properly, when you're ready."
> Then let her pick the time.
> A sorry closes your side. The honest thing opens hers.

> **On screen:** "we've been talking 3 weeks, she replies in 6 hours, I reply in 6 minutes. do I slow down?"
>
> **Said:** This is Say the Honest Thing. Today: playing the reply game.
> Don't slow down to match her. Matching her timing is a test, and she'll pass it or fail it without knowing she took one. Some people text slowly because their day is full, not because they're unsure about you. You can't read that from the clock.
> Ask once, plainly: "I like talking to you. Are you up for a proper date this week?" A yes tells you more than three weeks of timestamps.
> It has to be mutual, and you only find out by asking.

### Format B: "If ___, you're not ready to date yet"

Tyler's stat-and-twist shape, aimed at readiness instead of husbandhood. Always: a sourced number, "the last thing you want to do is ___", the series card, Hakeem's own proof, a rule.

> **On screen:** "if you ghost, you're not ready to date yet"
>
> **Said:** So I recently read that [stat on how common ghosting is + source]. But if you're trying not to be that person, the last thing you want to do is wait for a good time to say it. There isn't one.
> This is episode one of Read Before You Date.
> When I built Align, the one rule I wouldn't bend on was this: you can't just disappear. Letting someone go is one tap, and they're told kindly. Because ghosting is never about the other person. It's about not wanting to feel the ten seconds of saying it.
> So say it: "I enjoyed meeting you, and I don't see this going further. I wanted to tell you rather than fade."
> Release, don't haunt.

More hooks in this shape (each needs its stat sourced first):
- "if your dealbreakers come out on date five, you're not ready to date yet"
- "if you only date your sun sign's 'match', you're not ready to date yet" (one true trait per sign, never a verdict)
- "if you need to see her photo before you'll talk to her, you're not ready to date yet" (Align's veiled card)
- "if you can't name what you want, you're not ready to date yet"

### Format C: "Things I didn't realize I was doing wrong in dating (as someone building a dating app)"

The confession list, told by the founder. Two real examples from Hakeem: "I used to ___. I realized that's a problem because ___. Now ___." This is the one format only Hakeem can make: the examples must be true, so they're left for him to fill in.

### What to make first

1. **Three "Say the Honest Thing" videos.** This is Tyler's strongest format right now; it travels on comments, and Align's core line ("say the honest thing") is already the series name.
2. **Two "not ready to date yet" videos**, on the strongest topics (ghosting, dealbreakers), with real sources.
3. **One confession video** once Hakeem has two true examples.

Then post twice a week for four weeks in the same setting, and compare with `instagram/` trial reels before changing anything.

## Leave alone

- Stitches and rebuttals of other creators (his weakest posts).
- Pickup and approach advice.
- Anything that sounds like an ad, or like a percent match.
