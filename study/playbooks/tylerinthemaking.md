# @tylerinthemaking: playbook for Align

Studied 2026-11-05 from the pack in `hakeemawbrey/align-study`: 42 of the page's 43 posts, Feb 18 to Oct 22, 2026, 204K followers. Every transcript and caption was read. Contact sheets were checked for 6 posts (4 top, 2 bottom), not all 42.

Structure only. Nothing of Tyler's gets reused, in line with the `founder/` rule. Facts only Hakeem has stay in `[brackets]`.

## How far to trust this

- **Views added by hand.** The pack had no views, so they were read from his public Reels tab on Nov 5. They're rounded (2.4M, 803K) and show the total so far, so older reels have had longer to build up. Likes and views track closely (rank correlation 0.93). In the series table, "×" means views against the median reel (271.5K), and elsewhere it's likes against the median (about 16K) unless marked as views.
- **Not studied:** the Reels tab shows 2 reels the pack lacks (`DeXEa3cyY76`, 53.6K; `Dcl997sqiuU`, 37.1K), so there are no transcripts for them.
- **No comment text.** Comment counts exist, but what people said doesn't. Any "why it works" below is my reading, not something the data proves.
- **Small samples.** 42 posts, split further into series of 4 to 17.
- **Newer posts have had less time to collect likes,** which understates them if anything.

## What the page is

One idea, repeated: a man tells other men what they're getting wrong with women, using his long relationship with his fiancée as proof. It's almost entirely one format too: a 30 to 60 second talking head (90% of reels), fast speech (about 209 words a minute), words on screen from the first second.

## What works

### 1. The reader-question series is the strongest format

| Series | Posts | Median views | Views × | Likes × | Likes per 100 views | Range (views ×) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reader question (Gentleman's Corner) | 7 | 803K | **×3.0** | ×4.7 | 7.9 | 0.3 to 9.2 |
| Stat, then reversal (Modern Day Gentleman) | 17 | 364K | ×1.3 | ×1.1 | 5.1 | 0.2 to 16.9 |
| Confession (bias and misogyny series) | 4 | 336K | ×1.2 | ×2.6 | 11.7 | 0.4 to 6.6 |
| Essay or manifesto | 4 | 176K | ×0.65 | ×0.7 | 6.3 | 0.2 to 9.9 |
| Reaction to another creator | 5 | 99K | ×0.37 | ×0.4 | 4.3 | 0.3 to 3.7 |

- It first appears on Jul 27. Within three months it produced 3 of the page's 5 reels above 2M views.
- **Likes overstated the gain.** From the older half of posts to the newer half, median likes rose about 3.5× (9.3K to 32.9K) but median views only about 1.5× (241K to 364K). Newer reels earn more likes per view, so much of the jump is people liking more, not more people watching.
- **Reach is concentrated.** The top 6 reels hold 56% of all 28.9M views, so a typical reel is not the point. The page lives on outliers.
- **Confessions get liked more than they get seen.** They have the highest like rate (11.7 per 100 views) but only a typical median reach, so they read as loyal-audience content rather than reach content.
- The format has a shape: a dark message card with a boyfriend's complaint in his own defensive words, which he reads aloud. Then comes one sympathetic line, followed by the verdict that he's the problem.
- **My reading of why:** it plays out a real fight, and the viewer gets to see the guy corrected. That invites sending it to a partner. I can't test this without send data.
- The two weakest entries are the exceptions: ×0.9 was framed around money, and ×0.3 was the only one asked by a woman about her boyfriend. Two posts isn't proof, but keep the asker as the man in the wrong.

### 2. Every script runs on the same skeleton

**Stat series**
1. Headline on screen states the conflict, while the voice opens with a statistic. They're two different hooks at once.
2. Reversal: the obvious fix is the wrong one.
3. Series title, around 12 seconds in (median across 29 posts).
4. Personal proof from his own relationship.
5. A rule, often numbered.
6. A closing line that restates the rule.

**Reader-question series**
1. Message card, read aloud.
2. Series title and topic name.
3. One line of sympathy.
4. Verdict that reframes her behaviour.
5. One specific thing to do.
6. Closing line.

Giving each problem a name makes it feel like a concept worth sharing, such as the provider's trap or weaponized honesty.

### 3. The look is cheap and consistent

- The same room and the same painting behind him in all 6 sheets I checked, so a post is recognisable straight away.
- **He holds an odd object in 4 of the 6:** a spatula, a strainer, a fork, a toy camera. It's a curiosity beat that costs nothing, but one of the four is also the page's worst post, so it's a garnish and not the cause.
- Two main text styles: a clean serif headline, or a dark message card. Captions are small and sit low. The one big departure I saw, red text on a staged set, was the ×0.2 manifesto.
- Each script makes one point, and b-roll is rare: an occasional clip or GIF.

### 4. He re-shoots concepts, and results swing wildly

Same concept, different takes:

| Concept | Results |
| --- | --- |
| Housework | ×19.1, ×1.1 |
| Things I didn't realize (bias) | ×14.9, ×4.0, ×1.2, ×0.7 |
| Protecting her health | ×6.4, ×2.0, ×0.2 |
| The guy friend | ×2.9, ×1.7, ×0.8, ×0.7 |
| Kids and helping out | ×3.1, ×2.1 |

Likes undersold one of these. The guy-friend reels have a low like rate, but two reached far more people than their likes suggest: 1.4M views (×5.2 by views, ×2.9 by likes) and 798K (×2.9, ×1.7). People may watch this topic without wanting to be seen liking it.

Lesson: a flop doesn't kill an idea. Re-shooting is cheap and gives another shot at an outlier. He can't reliably predict winners either, since the same concept can move by 15× or more.

## What doesn't work

- **Manifesto and picking a fight** (×0.2): a big claim about him, with no problem for the viewer.
- **Tactics and tips** (pickup lines ×0.1, where to meet people ×0.1): the bottom two posts on the page.
- **Asking for follows** (×0.1, one post): too little to conclude from, but no reason to copy it.
- **Reactions and debunks:** they draw more than twice the comments per like, and views confirm they still don't travel (median 99K, ×0.37). The one exception reached 1M.

## For Align

**Why this fits.** Align is a dating app, and Hakeem can speak to this as a founder. The gap is proof: Tyler's authority is his seven-year relationship. Hakeem needs his own true source of authority, which could be what he's learned from building Align or from real users, or `[his own relationship story]`. Don't borrow a relationship angle that isn't true.

**Don't copy**
- His stats without checking. The same claim changes between reels: men catching feelings in friendships is 70% more likely in some takes and four times more likely in another. Every Align stat needs a source in hand.
- Made-up reader messages. Use real ones with permission, or label them as composites.
- His phrases (husband material, modern gentleman, Gentleman's Corner). The structure carries over, and the wording doesn't.
- Anti-swiping takes without thinking it through, since Align's own deck uses swipes. The honest angle is its limit of 15 people a night on Free.

**Make these, in this order**

1. **Second Look** (reader questions; lead format). Named after Align's real event card. Show the reader's message as a Stardust card: indigo surface, warm bone text. Close with one Talk card as the thing to do, which puts the product in without a pitch.
2. **What I got wrong** (confession). Two specific mistakes Hakeem made, ideally building a dating app. Needs real ones.
3. **Stat, then reversal** (sourced). Lower median, but it produced the page's biggest post, so use it as the occasional big swing.

**Skip for now:** manifestos, tips-only posts, follow requests.

**Set**
- One room and one repeated object, kept for every shoot. A card deck in hand makes the odd object into the brand.
- 40 to 55 seconds. Series title by 12 seconds.
- Run the voice rules from `docs/BRAND-QA.md`: plain, short sentences, no hype.

## Three draft scripts

Each is about 40 to 50 seconds at a normal pace. Everything in brackets must be real before filming.

### Second Look

**Screen (message card):** `[real reader message, e.g. a man whose girlfriend went quiet after he said he "just needed space"]`

> [Read the message aloud.]
> This is Second Look. Today, let's talk about `[named problem]`.
> I get it. `[one honest line of sympathy for him]`.
> But she didn't go quiet to punish you. `[what her silence is really telling him]`.
> Here's what to do tonight. Don't explain yourself again. Ask her one question and listen to the whole answer.
> The one I'd use: `[real Talk card question from First Nights or The Deep End]`.
> `[One-line rule that restates the fix.]`

**Caption:** the rule, in one or two sentences. Three or four hashtags.

### What I got wrong

**Screen:** 2 things I got wrong about dating (while building a dating app)

> I thought building a dating app meant I understood dating. Here are two things I got wrong.
> This is What I Got Wrong.
> Number one: `[real belief he had]`. I thought `[assumption]`. Then `[what actually happened, with a real detail]`. So now `[what changed in the app or in his behaviour]`.
> Number two: `[real belief]`. `[Same shape, ideally something a user told him.]`
> `[Closing line that names what both mistakes had in common.]`

### Stat, then reversal

**Screen:** `[conflict headline, one line, written for the person in the wrong]`

> So I recently read that `[stat, source on hand]`.
> But if you want to change that, the last thing you should do is `[the obvious fix]`.
> This is `[series name]`. Let's talk about `[named problem]`.
> When we built Align, `[true detail: e.g. why Free deals 15 people a night, from the real decision]`.
> So here's the rule. `[One specific rule.]`
> `[Closing line that restates it.]`

## Test before scaling

Film 9 over three to four weeks: 5 Second Look, 2 What I Got Wrong, 2 Stat. Re-shoot the best concept once with a new hook. Compare by **views and sends** from Align's own Instagram insights, not just likes. This study shows likes and reach can drift apart, and it has no send data.
