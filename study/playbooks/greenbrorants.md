# @greenbrorants (YouTube)

A study of the YouTube channel **@greenbrorants** (1.37M subscribers, 196
Shorts plus one long welcome video) and how Align can make videos in its
format.

**What this is built from.** Titles, view counts and the cover frame of every
Short. YouTube's bot wall blocked video downloads and transcripts from the
study machine, so there is no narration, pacing or audio here. Anything below
about how a story is told is an **inference** from titles and covers, and is
marked as one. No lines from the channel are quoted, because none were seen.

What it adds up to:

- **State the rule in the title.** Titles that state a rule (91) have a median
  of 7.0M and 28 Shorts over 10M. Bare noun titles (50) have 4.4M and 8.
- **The strongest rule has a cost.** "If you do this, that happens to you"
  is the best type by a wide margin. The cost is personal and physical: you
  shrink, you swap ages, you turn invisible, you are forgotten.
- **"In this world" belongs in the hook, not the title.** Only three titles
  use it, and they are middling. (Inference: the phrase is likely the spoken
  first line; the title just states the rule.)
- **Emoji:** 134 of 141 story titles end in an emoji; most use one (median
  6.0M), some stack two or three (6.9M). The seven with none have a 3.4M
  median. 🤯 is on six Shorts with an 11.5M median; 😱 is the most used (19)
  and only average (5.0M). Use one emoji that pictures the rule.
- **Length:** longer titles do better. 1–3 words: 4.1M median. 4–5: 5.3M.
  6–7: 7.1M. 8 or more: 7.9M. A full rule in plain words beats a teaser noun.
- **Title Case**, no punctuation except the cliffhanger type.

## How the stories work

The structure below is inferred from titles and covers, not from watching.

1. **One world rule**, stated in the first line (likely as "In this world…").
   It is concrete and visual: a window on your back, a string to the person
   you love most, the sun checking that everyone is awake.
2. **A young first-person narrator** living under it, usually a kid or teen
   at home, at school or in a small town.
3. **The rule's cost.** Someone pays for it: lying shrinks you, popping your
   balloon erases you, moving makes you vanish.
4. **A twist** that turns the rule around (several titles give it away:
   the monster under the bed was scared, the new kid was the moon).
5. **A payoff** that is either warm (grandparents, a parent, a friend) or
   eerie (Mr. Smiles, "Don't Turn Around", night versions of your family).

**Recurring motifs:** grandparents (4 titles, 7.5M median: "The Grandparent
Factory", "Grandma's Secret Trapdoor"); family (7 titles, 8.0M); the sky, sun,
moon, stars and clouds (9 titles, **12.0M** median, the strongest motif);
school and the lunch lady; the town; body and age swaps (3); lying penalties
(2, 15.1M median); love made visible ("A String Ties To Who You Love Most",
17M); playground games; golden objects; clowns; Mr. Smiles, a smiling villain
who returns.

**Visual language (from the covers):**

- **Protagonist:** a blocky avatar in a green shirt and a green three-eyed
  alien hat, in most story covers. Supporting cast: white-haired grandmas,
  kids, a lunch lady, a smiling sun, villains with painted grins.
- **Framing:** tight close-ups on the face, often chest-up and slightly low,
  with a wide establishing shot now and then.
- **Faces:** big flat decal faces (wide eyes, open mouth, tears, heart eyes)
  that carry all the emotion.
- **Light and place:** warm golden-hour or pink-dusk towns, suburban houses,
  school corridors with lockers, bedrooms at night. Saturated and soft.
- **Captions:** one word at a time, bold white sans with a black outline and
  shadow, lower-centre (about 70% down). Lowercase except names and "I".
  (Inference: the cover is simply a frame from the video, word included.)
  The early story era used yellow words; the list era used coloured words on
  the split.
- **Engine look:** a Roblox-style blocky world with modern lighting.

## What Align borrows, and what it never borrows

**Borrow (the format only):**

- A one-rule world, stated in the title, with a cost and a twist.
- First person, about 45–60 seconds, first line "In this world, …".
- One word on screen at a time, bold white with a dark outline, lower-centre.
- Close-ups on expressive faces in a warm, soft-lit small town.
- Title Case title that states the whole rule, plus one emoji.
- Sky motifs. They are the channel's strongest theme and Align's home turf.

**Never borrow:**

- Any character, name or look: not the green three-eyed-hat avatar, not Mr.
  Smiles, not the lunch lady, not their towns or sets.
- Any title, premise or line as written. Our rules are about love and
  honesty, and our twists are our own.
- Roblox assets, logos or the channel's audio and voice.
- Kid romance. The channel's narrators read young; Align's audience is 18+
  and dating, so every love story is between adults. Childhood can appear
  only as memory, never as romance.

Align's own world, characters and renderer live in `reels/world/`.

## Five Align stories

Voice as in `reels/town/README.md`, except narration uses normal sentence
case. No emoji in narration, no exclamation marks, no soulmate promises,
never "compatible". Astrology reads; it never decides. Each script uses at
most two Align ideas.

### 1. Your Card Only Flips If It's Mutual 🃏

*Idea: cards stay face down until it's mutual. Being rendered in
`reels/world/`.*

> In this world, everyone carries a card with their name on it, face down.
> It only flips when the person you like likes you back.
> For three years, mine never moved. I watched other cards turn at bus stops,
> at weddings, once in the middle of a fire drill.
> So I put mine in a kitchen drawer and stopped checking.
> Then the woman from downstairs started knocking. She'd borrow a ladder.
> Return the ladder. Borrow it again.
> Last night she asked, very quietly, if I ever looked at my card.
> I opened the drawer. It was face up. Her name was on the back.
> It had been face up since March.
> It has to be mutual. Sometimes it already is, and you're the one who
> stopped looking.

*Twist:* it flipped months ago. *Landing:* the last line.

### 2. If You Ghost Someone, You Become One 👻

*Idea: release, don't haunt.*

> In this world, if you ghost someone, you turn into one. Not forever. Just
> until you say goodbye properly.
> You can always tell. They're a little see-through at the grocery store.
> Their coffee goes cold faster.
> I swore I'd never be one. Then a man I'd seen four times sent me a long,
> kind message, and I didn't answer. Not because I was cruel. Because I
> didn't know how to say no.
> By Friday my hands were glass. By Sunday the elevator doors closed on me.
> Here's what nobody tells you. The person you ghost doesn't stay haunted.
> You do. He was fine by Tuesday.
> So I wrote back. Three sentences. Thank you. It's not for me. Good luck.
> My hands came back before I hit send.
> Release, don't haunt. It's lighter for both of you.

*Twist:* the ghost is the one who gets haunted.

### 3. Everyone Hears What You Mean 💬

*Idea: say the honest thing.*

> In this world, everyone hears what you mean, not what you say. You say
> nice weather, they hear please like me.
> Most people skip first dates entirely.
> I went anyway. I said I love hiking. He heard I'll pretend to love hiking
> if you do.
> He laughed. Then he said he'd hated every hike he'd ever been on.
> So we stopped performing. We said the real things. I want kids someday.
> I'm still a little sad about my last relationship. I read my horoscope
> before big decisions.
> It was the easiest date of my life.
> On the walk home he said, you know they lifted the rule in June, right?
> Nobody has heard anybody's meaning for months.
> We'd said all of it ourselves.
> Say the honest thing. It sounds better than you think.

*Twist:* the rule was gone; they were just honest.

### 4. Your Dealbreakers Are Painted On Your Door 🚪

*Ideas: dealbreakers up front; read the sky before the face (as a nod).*

> In this world, your dealbreakers are painted on your front door. You can't
> paint over them.
> Mine says: no smokers, must love dogs, will not move to the suburbs.
> For years I was embarrassed. People read my door, nodded politely and kept
> walking. My friends with blank doors seemed luckier.
> Then I learned what a blank door means. It isn't luck. It's someone who
> never decided. They find out on date nine. Or year three.
> Last spring a man stopped. He read my door before he looked at my face.
> Twice. Then he walked me across the street to his.
> No smokers. Two dogs. Will not move to the suburbs.
> We weren't perfect. We just never wasted a dinner finding out the obvious.
> Dealbreakers up front. It isn't cold. It's kind to both doors.

*Twist:* blank doors are not the lucky ones.

### 5. At 11:11 The Sky Deals Everyone A New Card 🕚

*Ideas: a new deal at 11:11; you won't arrive alone.*

> In this world, at 11:11 every night, the sky deals everyone a new card.
> Whatever went wrong that day goes back in the deck.
> People make wishes. I never did. A new deal just meant new ways to lose.
> So most nights at 11:10, I closed my curtains.
> Then one night the power went out across the whole town. Everyone came
> outside. Hundreds of us on the street, phones dead, no clocks.
> Nobody knew when 11:11 was. So we looked up and guessed together.
> The woman next to me said, I think it's now. It was.
> I didn't feel lucky. I felt counted.
> Turns out the deal comes whether you watch or not. I'd been getting new
> cards for years. I'd just been opening them alone.
> A new deal at 11:11. You won't arrive alone.

*Twist:* the new deal always came; what was missing was company.
